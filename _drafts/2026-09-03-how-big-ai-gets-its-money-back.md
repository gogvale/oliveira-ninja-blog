## Why this post exists

AI infrastructure spending is the largest coordinated investment in history, while token prices hit record lows. Both can't be "the story" forever — something has to give. This post lays out the competing theories for how the money comes back. Economics analysis, no vendor-bashing, no employer angle.

## The capex numbers (grounded, verified Feb-Apr 2026)

- Big-5 hyperscalers: **$600B+ capex 2026, +36% YoY; ~75% (~$450B) AI-tied** (MUFG Americas, Dec 2025: mufgamericas.com/sites/default/files/document/2025-12/AI_Chart_Weekly_12_19_Financing_the_AI_Supercycle.pdf)
- Estimates range $630B (Morgan Stanley via Reuters Breakingviews, Mar 2026) to **$775-800B** (AL Capital, Feb 2026: alcapitaladvisory.com/research/intelligence/ai-infrastructure.html)
- Company-level (Futurum, Feb 2026): Amazon ~$200B, Alphabet $175-185B, Meta $115-135B, Microsoft tracking similar
- Combined 2026 capex of Amazon/Google/Meta/Microsoft exceeds the GDP of all but the top-20 economies (tech-insider.org, Mar 2026 — use only as secondary)

## The seven theories (write-up material)

1. **The volume play (Jevons paradox)** — prices fall → usage explodes → total token revenue grows. Classic tech playbook (memory, bandwidth, compute all followed it). Question: does token demand have the same elasticity when marginal cost per token stays above zero and customers are businesses, not consumers?
2. **Cost deflation outruns price cuts** — inference efficiency (speculative decoding, distillation, better silicon) drops cost/unit faster than price. Margins widen while prices fall. Evidence to gather at write time: published inference-cost-per-token studies, hardware generation jumps.
3. **The shovel-seller play** — the labs may be loss leaders that exist to justify NVIDIA/hyperscaler/energy rents. NVDA, VRT, EQIX, CEG, MU capture the value chain (AL Capital's "five positions" thesis). AI = demand-creation engine for the real businesses.
4. **Value shifts up the stack** — model tokens commoditize; profits move to agents, vertical apps, subscriptions, and enterprise services built on top. Labs become platforms that monetize outcomes, not tokens. (Note: this is the "rent the tool" arc the blog already covers from the buyer side.)
5. **Financialized infrastructure** — data centers as a real-estate/utility play: long-term contracts, debt-funded buildout, power deals. Even if models never profit, the buildings/energy/power purchase agreements do. The "debt and defaults" bear case lives here.
6. **Subsidize now, price later** — the Uber playbook: below-cost pricing to kill competition, consolidation, then pricing power. Bear counterpoint: unlike rides, there's no network-effect moat and open weights keep a price floor.
7. **The sovereign/enterprise backstop** — government programs (Stargate-style, sovereign AI) and defense contracts underwrite demand that normal commercial usage never would. This is where "returns" stop being a market question.
