## Draft (story voice, ~900 words target)

**Scene 1 — the receipt and the headline.** I pay for a couple of AI subscriptions. Nothing fancy, the mid tier. Last month I went to cancel one and the renewal price made me stop: half of what I paid in January, for a model that did not exist in January. Better model, cheaper. Good deal for me.

Same week, some earnings headline crossed my feed: the big cloud providers are on track to spend more on AI infrastructure this year than the GDP of most countries. Six hundred billion, some say eight. I stared at both facts for a minute. Cheapest intelligence ever sold, biggest bet ever placed — in the same quarter. That math does not work unless somebody has a plan. So I started asking who.

**Scene 2 — the argument I keep having.** (Personal beat: rehash it with a work friend / the version in my head. Two sentences of dialogue max — sets up that this is a question I live with, not a thesis I'm selling.)

**Scene 3 — theory one: the bakery.** (Sell tokens cheap, sell more tokens. It worked for everything else that got cheap — storage, bandwidth, compute. The wrinkle I keep coming back to: businesses don't eat unlimited tokens like consumers eat unlimited video. Their appetite stops when the output stops paying. Volume has to beat value. Maybe it does. Maybe it doesn't.)

**Scene 4 — theory two: the gold rush.** (Nobody remembers the miners' names. The people who got rich sold the jeans, the picks, the claim maps. The buildout is real money — chips, racks, power plants, buildings. That money lands before a single token sells. If the whole AI bet goes sideways tomorrow, the shovel sellers already banked five record years. The capex is not a bet on AI. It is the product.)

**Scene 5 — theory three: the streaming lesson.** (When every service had the same movies, the money stopped following the movie and started following the bundle. Tokens are becoming the same movie. The survivors are the ones selling the outcome — agents, workflows, subscriptions — not the raw intelligence. I have written about the buyer side of this before. This is the seller side.)

**Scene 6 — the honest answer.** (Three theories, and I do not know which one wins. But each one predicts something I can check in a year: who is still selling tokens, whose margins moved, who quietly changed what they invoice. I am keeping score. Ask me in twelve months.)
